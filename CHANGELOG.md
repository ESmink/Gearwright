# Changelog

All notable changes are documented here. Each entry that changes persisted data must name its schema transition and migration test.

## 0.1.0 - unreleased
- Changed empty pipe sides and bottoms to one diagonal wooden brace. A second plank adds inset narrow-board crate lining while leaving pipe ends and fittings exposed. Insulated pipes have full-block collision, solid attachment faces, skylight blocking and wooden-wall room retention. Installing or removing the lining invalidates cached rooms immediately. Hydraulic schema 8 migrates additively to 9 with optional `woodInsulationStack`; schema-8 fixtures retain the original frame, contents and unknown fields through save/reload. Contracts exercise the installed engine's room registry on all six boundaries, lighting changes, protected malformed saves and exact returns of both planks and fittings. Graphics checks cover all 64 layouts with and without lining.
- Expanded the wooden pipe frame to all six block boundaries so adjacent and stacked frames meet continuously. Braces and endpoint struts follow the larger frame, and the top infill meets its upper rim without overlapping surfaces. Collision and selection bounds follow the expanded wood; sparse tops, barrel openings and saved addon state are unchanged.
- Added a whole-pipe wooden support addon fitted with one plank. The selected C design uses endpoint collars on pipe faces, corner braces on empty faces, and local feet against walls or ground. Its sparse top supports barrels when no pipe is above; an upward terminal connection omits the middle plank. Hydraulic schema 7 migrates sequentially to schema 8 with an optional `woodSupportStack`, retaining the original plank and all existing fields. Runtime fixtures load schemas 1, 3 and 7 through save/reload, preserve malformed or newer data unchanged, and verify claim checks, server-only installation, barrel support and single-item returns for every pipe addon. Graphics checks cover all 64 port layouts and nozzle clearance in all six directions. The model reviewer now preserves shared states when switching candidates.
- Increased pipe and pump intake conductance another fourfold (8 L/s/kPa for liquid, 1.6 standard L/s/kPa for gas) and reduced the pump's swept chamber from 8 L to 4 L. Pump discharge and air outlet rates retain their previous tuning. Pressurized-feed runtime tests fill each intake through three charged pipes at 0.5–4 RPS with 20 kPa supply and at 8 RPS with 40 kPa, in both directions. Sparse preconditioning and bounded valve-corner handling keep faster full lines converging without stroke pauses. Gravity, suction, gas compression, 10 L pipes and literal saved amounts are preserved; no saved fields, schemas or model geometry changed.
- Increased liquid and gas pipe conductance fourfold, including pump ports, container connections, natural intakes and air outlets. Side/down liquid outlets now have an 8 L/s baseline. Flow still depends on pressure, height, available contents and downstream resistance; pipe capacity, compression, suction thresholds and simulation cadence are unchanged. The gravity-fed full-line regression delivers over 20 L in ten seconds while retaining exact item conservation. Saved fields and schemas are unchanged.
- Reworked pipe flow to solve connected pipe storage, piston chambers and pressure-driven outlets together in bounded 20 ms steps. Full liquid pipes can receive and discharge in the same step; small retained excesses provide stiff compression and real back-pressure instead of blocking every full receiver. Gravity and powered suction remain available, while gases retain compressible standard-litre storage and ignore liquid gravity head. Natural-source intake retains its -70 kPa threshold. Topology discovery is cached, failed components retry with backoff, and simulation updates are throttled to 10 Hz. Runtime regressions cover full-line pump discharge and capped-line stalls in both directions, lifted dry priming, gas flow, conservation, chunk pauses and protected saves. Hydraulic schema 7, pump schema 1, saved fields, asset codes and approved geometry are unchanged.
- Fixed blocked reciprocating pumps coasting through bottom dead centre on larger or geared drivetrains, especially with a nearly empty chamber. Pump networks now integrate cached motor torque against fluid resistance in bounded time and angle substeps, preserving the normal torque/flywheel sampling cadence. Braking can consume the remaining speed after inertia scaling; a finite load does not impose an angle lock. Engine regressions cover 0.1 L and 4 L fills, 16/128 nodes, 1:1/4:1 gearing, both directions, delayed ticks, stable stops, outlet release, expansion startup, and sufficient torque to overcome the load. Saved fields, schemas, assets, and approved geometry are unchanged.
- Synchronized pumped mechanical networks, piston poses, wet checks, and chamber contents through one server snapshot clock, including the final stopped angle. Mechanical motion uses bounded substeps; fluid exchange now shares the 20 ms pipe solve, with presentation snapshots published after both updates. Load is evaluated before movement across the upcoming stroke, remains finite above service pressure, and no longer brakes expansion with trapped positive pressure. Liquid presentation holds its amount during dry piston travel. Runtime tests cover both drive directions, blocked-outlet stalls and recovery, exact transfer conservation, upward overflow, client packet interpolation, and pump removal; saved fields, schemas, asset codes, and approved geometry are unchanged.
- Prevented passive liquid columns from turning the empty-pipe pressure bias into increasing vacuum at higher elevations. Suction propagation no longer gains pressure from height, while source lift still consumes water head and downhill flow remains gravity-driven. Driven suction is now a transient per-pipe cache rebuilt from pump boundaries, so old saved pressure cannot prime free natural-source extraction. Tests cover 32-block passive columns, horizontal powered suction, source lift, and existing long pump circuits; saved fields and schemas are unchanged.
- Removed fluid-loss paths in pump and pipe storage: trace remainders retain their identity, over-capacity liquid is kept rather than trimmed, and networks with read-only receivers pause before debiting donors. Pump resistance now rises smoothly and steeply above its 1500 kPa service pressure without a forced shaft lock; trapped contents remain stored even if external power keeps moving the piston. A stationary pressurized chamber can discharge after its outlet becomes available. Liquid rendering meets the basin's inner walls and floor and fills the space beneath the live piston. Pressure is recomputed when saved/client state is read. Runtime conservation and geometry-fit tests cover these changes; no saved fields, schemas, or approved model parts changed.
- Fixed dry intake runs discarding their priming vacuum and filled pipes remaining pinned to that vacuum, which starved reciprocating pumps on longer source connections. Discharge now stops at outlet pressure equilibrium, so liquid delivery follows piston displacement instead of dumping the reservoir in bursts. Added a translucent liquid top joined to the chamber's window panes and bounded below the piston. Regression circuits cover long intakes, a one-block source lift, reverse rotation, mass conservation, and gradual exhaust under back-pressure. Saved fields, schemas, the source suction threshold, and runtime model geometry are unchanged.
- Fixed reciprocating pumps drawing water into the inlet pipe but never filling their chamber. Pipe-to-chamber and chamber-to-pipe transfers now compare against the pipe pressure before that pump's own boundary is imposed, instead of cancelling their pressure difference. Other sources and pumps still supply back-pressure, and transfers remain bounded by actual contents and receiving space. Regression contracts cover dry priming, reservoir filling, discharge, reverse rotation, gases, and retaining compressed liquid against a full outlet. No saved fields or schemas changed.
- Corrected the Reciprocating Drive Shaft's rotation handedness to match the vanilla axle renderer's signed axis, including when its lone axle connection changes ends. The pump now directly transforms separate piston, rod, and check-disc meshes instead of scrubbing an independent animation. Both rod bearings stay attached throughout the full six-unit piston stroke; mount-aware journal phase drives rendering, chamber volume, and load for downward, upward, and sideways pumps. Chamber volume and content clipping follow the exact slider-crank travel. Existing shapes, animation codes, and schema-1 saved state remain compatible.
- Fixed the Reciprocating Drive Shaft's live rotation by applying the mechanical-network angle directly to one static mesh instead of maintaining a second animation state. The wooden axle, crank web, and journal now share exactly one transform, while runtime model tests verify that the pump connecting rod remains pinned to the circular journal and sliding piston crosshead throughout the complete cycle.
- Fixed Reciprocating Drive Shaft placement. Its dashed public asset code now resolves both horizontal variants without being truncated by Vintage Story's single-part variant helper, and the dynamically rendered block has explicit selection and collision bounds.
- Added the `gearwright:lateral-crank-*` Reciprocating Drive Shaft and `gearwright:reciprocating-pump` single-acting pressure vessel. Each drive shaft stores an independent schema-1 45-degree journal offset, shows a cantilevered one-axle form unless connected through, and accepts one pump on any perpendicular face. The pump may be placed before its drive and starts working after a correctly aligned shaft is added. - Added the `gearwright:overrunningtransmission-*` block as a one-way automatic coupling. Its designated input and output remain separate Vanilla mechanical networks; the server normalizes both speeds to a live operating direction and reuses the bounded controlled-clutch exchange only while the input leads. A faster output or stopped input freewheels without back-driving the source in either rotational direction. Reverse operation mirrors the approved four-spoke oak input wheel's fifteen brass ramps and locking shoulders together with its three broad-arm tin-bronze pawls and leaf springs; the frame, hubs, shafts, and network angles remain unchanged. Runtime rendering uses separate live input, output, pawl, and spring meshes, derives lift from directed relative tooth phase at every speed combination, and keeps all three pawls synchronized because fifteen teeth divide evenly across their 120-degree spacing. The block and coordinator derive directly from generic mechanical and block-entity bases, so no inherited Vanilla transmission mesh renders inside the frame. The recipe now uses one brass and one tin-bronze plate. This is additive and introduces no persisted schema.
- Fixed the Overrunning Transmission's white brass wear parts by resolving the installed sheet through the engine-facing `game:` texture domain. The coordinator also tracks angular lag at the locked tooth and raises bounded, equal-and-opposite contact exchange to a fourfold ceiling before the input tooth can phase through a loaded pawl. The transient lock state is not persisted and resets whenever either terminal network changes; no save schema changes.
- Replaced the behavior assigned to the vanilla `game:transmission-ns`, `game:transmission-we`, and four `game:clutch-*` variants with a Gearwright-controlled clutch boundary. Each transmission end remains in its own vanilla mechanical network; engaging the clutch applies a bounded, equal-and-opposite server-side speed exchange without merging, splitting, or rebuilding those networks. Terminal positions are resolved after Vintage Story assigns the owning block position, and a valid lone output shaft receives its own network even though the transmission is hidden from vanilla discovery. Topology discovery waits for the first server tick so neighbouring block entities have initialized their Vanilla mechanical-power managers. The clutch drum reads the adjacent shaft angle instead of the intentionally networkless transmission. Synchronization now accelerates with larger speed differences while remaining bounded. This is the step-3 test implementation; component-specific inertia and a compact flywheel are not included yet.
